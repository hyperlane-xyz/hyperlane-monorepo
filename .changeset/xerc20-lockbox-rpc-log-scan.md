Two things break. The explorerUrl and apiKey fields are dropped from the GetExtraLockboxesOptions type, and HyperlaneJsonRpcProvider no longer answers an eth_getLogs request it cannot serve in full over a narrower window, throwing LogBlockRangeTooLargeError instead.

getExtraLockBoxConfigs is routed through EvmEventLogsReader instead of calling the block explorer API directly, so chains without a usable explorer now scan ConfigurationChanged logs over the RPC rather than silently returning no extra bridges. The scan is paginated with a 1_000_000 block range measured against base.

EvmXERC20Reader.readOnChainBridges is routed through EvmEventLogsReader as well, where it used to call eth_getLogs directly from block 0 to the head. It now reads over the block explorer where the chain has a usable one, starts at the block the xERC20 was deployed in rather than at genesis, and paginates with the same block range as getExtraLockBoxConfigs. The direct call carried no pagination of its own and so was left to whatever the transport made of a full history span: on a chain whose endpoint rejects a query that wide the read failed outright, and it could also come back with the logs of a narrower window and nothing to say the rest had been dropped.

The RPC log pagination backing EvmEventLogsReader now requests chunks that span exactly the configured range instead of one block more, halves the range when a provider rejects a chunk and reuses the reduced range for the remaining chunks. The default range is unchanged at 500 blocks. A range below one block is rejected up front.

A failure that rejects the caller rather than the query is rethrown at once, without a retry and without shrinking the range, because neither can change the answer and the ladder below would otherwise cost four attempts and their backoffs at every range down to one block. Such a failure is recognised by the isRecoverable === false flag retryAsync already honours, read through the same nested error walk, or by a 401 or 403 answer from the RPC.

Of the failures that remain, a rejection that names a block range or a result limit shrinks the range immediately. Every other one, such as a rate limit, a timeout or an outage, is first retried up to three times at the unchanged range with an exponential backoff, and only once that budget is spent is the range halved and the budget reset for the smaller range, so an unrecognised phrasing of a range rejection costs retries rather than the whole read. The original provider error is rethrown unchanged once the retries are spent at the one block minimum. The reduction is kept for the rest of the scan, so a transient failure used to degrade it permanently: ten of them took a one million block range down to 976.

HyperlaneJsonRpcProvider serves the whole block range a getLogs request asks for, issuing as many sub-queries of the RPC's declared pagination.maxBlockRange as that takes, still five at a time. A request spanning more than ten of them used to be answered by raising the start block and returning the logs of a narrower window, with nothing in the response marking the blocks that had been dropped, so EvmXERC20Reader.readOnChainBridges lost every bridge whose only ConfigurationChanged event sat below that floor. A cap on the sub-queries one request may cause is kept, and raised from 10 to 2000, but exceeding it now throws instead of narrowing the window: SmartProvider moves on to the next RPC, and the error is recognised as a block range rejection, by its type as well as by its message, so a paginating caller halves its chunks and completes the scan. The cap is not fitted to any chain's history, because a registry maxBlockRange is the value a chain declares rather than the one its endpoints enforce: avalanche declares 100000 on an endpoint that rejects a span of 2048 blocks. The cap is only reachable on an RPC that declares a maxBlockRange, and every scan in this package wide enough to reach one goes through getLogsFromRpc, which halves its own range on the rejection and carries on, so where the cap sits decides how finely a very deep scan ends up split rather than whether it completes.

The deduplication of ConfigurationChanged events is shared by getExtraLockBoxConfigs and EvmXERC20Reader.readOnChainBridges through latestConfigurationPerBridge, which orders the events of a bridge by block number and then by log index and keys them by the bridge's normalized address. getExtraLockBoxConfigs compared block numbers alone, so of two configurations of the same bridge in one block it kept the first instead of the last.

A block explorer that has no record of a contract's deployment transaction is no longer re-queried by the reader's retry loop, so the fallback to the RPC happens on the first response instead of after the retry budget is exhausted.

The deployment block number reported by the getcontractcreation explorer endpoint is now parsed and returned by getContractDeploymentTransaction, and EvmEventLogsReader uses it instead of fetching the deployment transaction receipt. This removes one RPC request per contract on every chain and makes log reads work on chains whose RPCs prune old receipts, where the receipt lookup returned null and forced a fallback to the RPC based binary search. The field is accepted both as the decimal string returned by Etherscan and Blockscout and as the JSON number returned by other explorers, and the receipt lookup is still used for explorers that omit it.
