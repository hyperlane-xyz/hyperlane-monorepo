Routers over Aevo xERC20 tokens were given a `TokenStandard` of their own, `EvmHypAevoXERC20`, which broke an exhaustive `Record<TokenStandard, ...>` or `switch` over it, and an SDK without it did not parse a route config that carries it. `EvmHypAevoXERC20Adapter` reported the limits of such a router like those of any other xERC20 router, from its authority, its cap and the amount it had minted and not burned, which it read through the undocumented getter behind the exported `AEVO_OUTSTANDING_SELECTOR`. What the router had bridged was that amount, not the supply of the token. The authority, the cap and that amount were read at one block, the latest one as `getBlock('latest')` reported it, so that a block that changes them while they are read was not seen in part, and `getLimits()` returned the four limits of the router from them. The burn maximum of a router with a cap was its cap, or the amount minted and not burned where that was beyond the cap, as the token bounds a burn by that amount and not by the cap, so the burn limit never exceeded it. The amount minted and not burned was read only where a limit needed it: the mint maximum, which the authority and the cap alone decide, and the mint limit and the burn maximum of a router without a cap did not read it, so a failure to read it did not fail them. An Aevo router needed no approval of its token for itself from the sender, as the burn of the token took no allowance.

`warp deploy` and a `warp apply` that adds a chain wrote the standard of each EVM xERC20 router from its token, as the exported `deriveEvmXERC20Standard` gave it: `EvmHypAevoXERC20` for an Aevo token, and `EvmHypVSXERC20` for a Velodrome token, which was written as `EvmHypXERC20` before. For a re-exported Velodrome route, `WarpCore` and the rebalancer then limited a transfer to half of the buffer cap of its router, and its balance and limits metrics were labelled `EvmHypVSXERC20` where they were labelled `xERC20` and `EvmHypXERC20`, with the router as the `wallet_address` of its balance metric in place of the xERC20 token. The standards of the routers were resolved before any token router was deployed and reused for the registry config.

`warp read` logged the limits of an Aevo router in the same table as the other xERC20 routers, read through `getLimits()`. It warned of an xERC20 router whose limits it could not read and left it out of the table, instead of failing before it derived any config. `getXERC20Info` of `@hyperlane-xyz/metrics` read an Aevo token through `getLimits()`, so that its four limits came from one block.

`deriveXERC20TokenType` and `deriveEvmXERC20Standard` threw an `Error`, not an `UnknownXERC20TypeError`, for a token behind an EIP-1967 proxy whose implementation had no bytecode, as they did for a token address without any, instead of reading it as a token of no known type. `warp read` and `warp check` failed for a route whose xERC20 proxy had an implementation without bytecode, where they had warned and skipped its xERC20 config. `deriveEvmXERC20Standard` threw an `Error` naming the token and the selector for an Aevo token without the getter behind `AEVO_OUTSTANDING_SELECTOR`, instead of answering `EvmHypAevoXERC20` for a router whose limits and bridged supply could not be read, so that `warp deploy` and a `warp apply` that adds a chain failed for such a token before deploying any router.

`contractDouble`, the typed partial test double of a contract, was exported from `@hyperlane-xyz/sdk` for the tests of other packages.
